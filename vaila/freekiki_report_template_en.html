4. The physics of the ball, explained

4.1 The three forces

After the kick, the ball is under three forces. Gravity pulls it towards the ground, always at 9.81 m/s². Drag is the force of the air opposing the motion: it slows the ball down. The Magnus effect is the force that curves the ball: when it spins, the air is pushed to one side and the ball is pushed to the other. In the figure, the bars show the size of each force at three moments of the kick.

4.2 The drag crisis

The air reacts differently depending on the speed. For a football, the drag coefficient (Cd) is about 0.47 at lower speeds and drops to something between 0.12 and 0.20 at higher speeds. This drop is the drag crisis: the layer of air that clings to the ball becomes turbulent, separates further back and leaves a smaller wake, so the ball meets less resistance. In this kick, the Reynolds number of the ball stayed between {{re_min}} and {{re_max}} × 10⁵ (see the figure in section 3). According to Asai & Seo (2013), the crisis happens at about 2 to 3 × 10⁵: above that, the air layer is already turbulent.

The physics (forces, drag and Magnus) follows the references, but this test shows that there are still differences of convention or of parameters.

7. Glossary

Drag
the force of the air that opposes the motion of the ball.
Cd
drag coefficient: how much the shape of the ball and the air layer resist the motion.
Drag crisis
the drop of Cd when the air layer changes from laminar to turbulent.
Magnus effect
a force perpendicular to the motion, caused by the spin; it is what curves the ball.
Reynolds number
the ratio between the inertia and the viscosity of the air; it shows the flow regime.
Spin parameter (Sp)
how much the ball spins compared with how fast it moves.
DLT3D
the camera calibration that links points of the pitch, in metres, to pixels of the image, frame by frame.
rps
revolutions per second.
