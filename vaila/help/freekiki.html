Correct and retrain (3 steps)

  1. Detect, then Correct in getpixelvideo (section 5). Or, in getpixelvideo, press Tpl: and type L (FreeKiki Load). Open the run or batch folder and press Enter: it finds the original video and opens it in correction mode (CLI: getpixelvideo.py --freekiki-run DIR). The predicted labels appear, in edit mode.
  2. Fix the frames you want to use:
    • Right-click picks a point and left-click places it.
    • F10 accepts a ghost.
    • A point inside the picture but not visible: Del (this frame) or Del Range (many frames). Points outside the picture are ignored.
    • F3 Frame OK accepts the frame; PgDn goes to the next draft.
    • X / Discard drops a bad frame (advert, wrong camera). It is never exported and disappears from navigation (arrows, slider, playback, PgDn). Shift+X shows discarded frames again; X restores one.
    • F9 Save dataset writes images/, labels/, data.yaml and session.json into the folder you choose. Only complete frames are saved.
  3. Section 3: Corrections → Add folder…, then Train (base active = fine-tune).
    • The folders are added to train once, with the ingest checks, and then training starts.
    • The optional Match id defaults to the video name.
    • CLI: train -w WS --base active --add-dataset FOLDER [--match-id M], without --manifest.

For each frame the field camera (fitted to the accepted points) says whether a needed keypoint is in the picture, even when the network misses it. Priority:

  1. in the picture and missed by the network;
  2. only with --half-seen: no camera, but half-seen (off by default: such frames were all discarded in review);
  3. in the picture and already accepted.

At most 5 frames per video (--per-video), --min-gap apart: many videos, few frames each.

--montage writes WS/incoming/montage_<ts>/:

  • montage.mp4: H.264 with every frame a key frame; other sizes are letterboxed;
  • session.json: the drafted predictions;
  • montage_frames.csv: source video, frame, time, scale/offset, reason and match.

match defaults to the source video name; cuts of one video share it. Edit it to merge the clips of one match. ingest without --match-id uses it per frame, so the leakage checks work per match.

Label every visible point, or hide it. An empty point is written as 0 0 0 ("not visible"), and the pose loss then teaches the network that the point is absent. A frame with only a few labelled points therefore makes the missed points worse.

FreeKiki checks every frame. The labelled pitch points give a field homography, and every point whose projection falls inside the image must be labelled or hidden with Del (occluded or not there). Frames that fail the check:

  • Mark Reviewed (F3) refuses them and names the missing points.
  • Export (F9) leaves them as drafts and lists them in incomplete_frames.csv.
  • ingest rejects them.

The check needs at least 4 spread pitch points.

Ghosts in the review window:

  • A cyan circle is a low-confidence AI point; its label shows the confidence.
  • A pink cross is the projection of the labelled points.
  • F10 (Accept ghost) copies the ghost of the selected point and moves to the next missing one.
  • The panel line missing: ... turns green (complete) when the frame can be exported.

Queue. queue picks up to 25 frames per clip, at least 5 frames apart, in this order:

  1. frames that do not calibrate;
  2. frames where p5/p29/p39/p47 are half-seen;
  3. the rest.

PageDown/PageUp walks exactly the queue. queue.csv says why each frame was chosen.

Train or hard. --split hard is a labelled holdout of difficult footage. It is never trained on; report it with evaluate --split hard and choose on val. A match lives in one split only:

  • train refuses val/test/hard matches and near-duplicates of their images;
  • hard refuses train/val/test matches and near-duplicates of train frames;
  • so a copy of a holdout clip cannot leak into training.

Before ingesting.

  • Preview validates every PNG/TXT pair.
  • Commit appends the pairs and publishes the manifest last.
  • Use one match ID across related cameras and cuts.
  • Val/test stay unchanged; a hard commit only adds hard: images/hard to the workspace data.yaml.
  • human_vs_ai.csv describes training annotations, not independent evaluation.

What it does

FreeKiki teaches a YOLO-pose network to find the 49 soccer-field keypoints of the kiki template (vaila/models/soccerfield_kiki.csv, skeleton vaila/skeletons/soccerfield_kiki49.json) in broadcast video — line intersections, penalty-arc intersections, goal posts and nets, corner flags and the centre — and then finds them in your videos. These points are what a later step needs to calibrate the camera frame by frame; FreeKiki already checks, per frame, whether the accepted points give a geometrically valid field homography (RANSAC, inliers, reprojection error, orientation). Compared with Field KPs (AI) (32-point FIFA/Roboflow schema), FreeKiki uses the richer 49-point schema with 3D-aware points (posts and flags, z ≠ 0).

If the training stops (Stop, crash, power loss) — resume it

What is saved. At the end of every epoch Ultralytics writes runs/<run>/weights/last.pt (model and optimizer state, epoch number) and one row in runs/<run>/results.csv. If the computer switches off in the middle of epoch 38, epochs 1–37 are safe; only the unfinished epoch is repeated.

When it ends the run is registered and promoted into its size slot (models/freekiki_<slot>.pt) if it is better — exactly as a normal Train.

  • Choose a specific run with resume -w WS --name RUN.
  • A run started with --manifest vNNN resumes on the same oversampled train list (the manifest is re-checked against the current labels first).
  • --device 0 or --batch 8 may be changed on resume (e.g. after an out-of-memory error); everything else comes from the run.
  • A workspace copied to another machine can be resumed there: the dataset path and run folder are taken from the current workspace.
  • Do not press Train again for an interrupted run: it would start a new run from epoch 1. FreeKiki refuses a Train that would overwrite a resumable or running run of the same name.
  • A run that finished (all epochs, or early stop by patience) cannot be resumed — its optimizer is removed. To train more, use Retrain (Base model active).

Workflow in detail

  1. Init workspace — creates the layout. Safe to repeat; never deletes.
  2. Import (copy) — copies data.yaml, images/, labels/, manifest.csv, keypoints_kiki49.csv, cameras.jsonl, reports/ of a kiki49 build. The source is only read; re-running resumes. data.yaml path: is rewritten to the new absolute folder (refreshed before each training).
  3. Check dataset — kpt_shape [49, 3], flip_idx/kpt_names equal to the CSV, per-split counts, label columns (5 + 147).
  4. Train
    • Smoke preset: yolo26n-pose.pt, 5 epochs, 10 % of the images, imgsz 640. Only proves the pipeline works; the model is weak.
    • Full preset (recommended real run): yolo26m-pose.pt, 150 epochs, imgsz 1280, batch -1 (AutoBatch), patience 30.
    • Retrain: base active fine-tunes the best model so far with AdamW, lr0=1e-4, lrf=0.01, one warmup epoch, cosine schedule and mosaic=0. The backbone stays trainable. A first train from yolo26*-pose.pt keeps optimizer=auto.
    • Build oversampling manifest: repeat-factor sampling (LVIS) of rare keypoints into an immutable manifests/vNNN/. The dataset is not modified. Default t = 0.05, cap 4, seed 0. A keypoint seen in a fraction f of train images repeats max(1, sqrt(t / f)) times; each image follows its rarest visible point. Put v001 in Manifest, or train --manifest v001. Val and test stay the original split. The first training on that list may rebuild labels/train.cache (derived; images and labels stay as they are).
    best.pt goes to models/registry.csv, is evaluated on val and compared with the model of its size slot; models/freekiki_<slot>.pt is replaced only when the configurable gate passes (see Model slots and Model promotion).
  5. Evaluate model — measures a model on a labelled split: val to choose thresholds and compare models, test only for the final report.
  6. Detect — runs the active (or any) model on one video or a whole folder.

How to read the quality numbers

Evaluate (labelled split — the objective measure)

Use --split val to choose thresholds and compare models. Keep test for the final report only: every look at test that changes a decision makes it less independent (sweep refuses a test evaluation unless --allow-test).

Choosing thresholds (val only)

sweep re-applies a grid of box (--det-confs) and keypoint (--kp-confs) thresholds to the saved predictions.npz and writes sweep.csv (recall, precision, false points, pck*_all, share of images with a valid and a correct homography). It shows the trade-off; pick on val, never on test.

Model slots (one promoted model per network size)

freekiki.toml keeps one promoted model per size in [models.<slot>] (file, backend, arch, base, run, pose_map50_95, pck10_all, sha256). Slots are n s m l x (YOLO26-pose, file models/freekiki_<slot>.pt) and hm_n..hm_x (heatmap backend). [models] default says which slot the name active means; switch it with models --default l. models lists the slots (* marks the default).

[models]
default = "m"

file = "models/freekiki_m.pt"

pose_map50_95 = 0.8576
sha256 = "..."
  • Migration from the old [active] table. The first time a schema-1 workspace is loaded, models/active.pt is copied to models/freekiki_m.pt (the SHA-256 of the copy is checked) and the old table is kept as [legacy_active]. active.pt stays on disk and is only read. The migration runs once; if freekiki_m.pt already exists with other content it stops with an error instead of overwriting it.
  • Sizes do not mix. The size (slot) of a checkpoint is read from the checkpoint itself. A run competes only with the model of its own slot; an empty slot takes its first run when its best-epoch pose mAP50-95 is above [promotion] new_slot_min_pose_map50_95 (0.5). sizes shows why weights cannot move between sizes: an m checkpoint matches 94 % of l's parameters by name and shape, but only 45 % of n, 57 % of s and 5 % of x, and a shape match between different widths is not a semantic match. So n, s and x start from the official yolo26{n,s,x}-pose.pt.
  • Grow m → l. m and l have the same width; l is deeper. grow copies every m tensor into l, zero-pads the extra input channels of the fusion convs and starts the new attention block as the identity, so the grown l computes the same function as m before training (the maximum output difference on a random image is printed and stored in the checkpoint; on the kiki49 m it was 5.5e-4 px). The grown file is only an initialisation: train it (train --base models/freekiki_l_init.pt, a continued fine-tune) and it fills the l slot only through the gate below.
  • Any base that is not an official yolo26*-pose.pt (a slot, a run's best.pt, a grown init) is trained as a continued fine-tune: AdamW, lr0 1e-4 with cosine decay, one warmup epoch, mosaic=0.

Model promotion (candidate vs its slot)

After train, the new best.pt is evaluated on val and compared with the model of its own slot on the same images (compare). It is copied to models/freekiki_<slot>.pt only if the gate passes; every decision (both model hashes, reasons) is appended to models/promotion_log.csv. Candidate weights are always kept in models/. Configure it in freekiki.toml:

[promotion]
mode = "gate"            # gate | map (old: pose mAP only) | never

Snap keypoints onto painted lines (detect default, or standalone snap)

On cameras rare in the training set (high, lateral, close to the box) raw network detections can exhibit a systematic 20-30 px offset on box corners. snap refits straight lines to the painted field markings and moves corners to line intersections and arc crossings along projective line directions. Stabilisation compares per-frame snap vs temporal running-mean correction to eliminate jitter while leaving known cameras untouched.

detect now runs snap by default (opt-out with --no-snap):

  • field_kps_getpixelvideo.csv contains the clean snapped keypoints on the paint.
  • Homography and DLT calibration (.dlt2d, .dlt3d) are fitted directly to the snapped points, improving camera accuracy.
  • The output overlay MP4 and snapshot display clean green markers on the paint, discarding the inferior un-snapped network shifts (red mode).
  • Raw network predictions remain preserved in field_kps_raw.csv.
  • snap_moves.csv logs every shifted point with dx, dy, and distance.

For existing runs, standalone freekiki snap can be run:

uv run --no-sync vaila/freekiki.py snap -w $W --run DETECT_BATCH_OR_RUN

Cameras the network was not trained for (detect skips them)

The network is trained on wide broadcast cameras. A camera behind the goal, on a player or zoomed in gives few keypoints, or confident ones that fit no pitch. A folder detect therefore screens every video first: 24 sampled frames are judged like select judges a frame (at least 6 accepted points, a camera fitted to them with an rmse of at most 5 px and a height of 8-45 m). A video where fewer than 30 % of the sampled frames have a valid pitch camera is skipped (nothing is deleted) and listed in skipped_videos.csv. A single video you name is still processed, with a warning. --no-screen runs everything; --min-camera-ok 0.3 sets the share. The GUI shows a warning box at the end of a batch that skipped videos.

Keypoints by hand (a skipped video, or a camera the network was not trained for)

When the network cannot see the camera (for example a view behind the goal), mark the 49 points yourself. Click Mark keypoints in section 5, or run getpixelvideo.py -f <video> --freekiki --freekiki-workspace <workspace>: the window opens on the video with the 49 points of the Soccer-Kiki template. Mark the points you can see (a few are enough for the camera; the field geometry fills the rest), then save from the Save menu as the markers CSV, not with F9 (F9 exports a FreeKiki session). It writes <video>_kiki49.csv next to the video. The ball stays in <video>_markers.csv. Then run Detect + 3D again: a video with its <video>_kiki49.csv is not screened, and its points are your marks. From the CLI: freekiki.py detect --video <video> --keypoints-csv <video>_kiki49.csv.

For a hand-marked video no model is loaded and the points are not snapped, so a mark stays where you put it. A point you did not mark has status A; the field camera fills it (status G). quality.json says "keypoint_source": "manual" and README.txt names the CSV. The file layout is frame,p0_x,p0_y,...,p48_x,p48_y in the pixels of the video; a file with another layout, or a frame outside the video, is refused. Limits: a hand mark has no network confidence, and a camera fitted from few points is less certain. Check the per-frame reprojection (field_kps_geom_status.csv, rmse_px) before you trust the 3D.

3D chain (detect-3d: DLT3D focal, SAM 3D Body, ball, players and C3D on the pitch)

Run it on a finished detect run or batch folder (it needs that run's .dlt3d and .dlt2d): freekiki.py detect-3d -w WS --run DETECT_RUN_OR_BATCH --weights-dir WEIGHTS --players-on-field --field-min-frac 0.5 --ball net. --ball takes off (default), net (the dedicated ball detector, <workspace>/models/ball.pt) or sam3 (the SAM 3 prompt, vaila/models/sam3/sam3.pt).

  • Focal per frame. The focal length of each frame's DLT3D is written as cam_int.npy and passed to sam3dinov3 (--cam-int-npy). The principal point stays at the image centre: the full decomposition is unstable on broadcast footage. Frames without a DLT3D use the default intrinsics.
  • People. sam3dinov3 runs with the SAM 3 text prompt --text (default person). With --players-on-field a person is kept when the feet (bottom centre of the box) lie inside the pitch for at least --field-min-frac of its frames. The filter runs on the finished output (players_on_field.csv, players_on_field_camera.csv, poses_3d_on_field.csv).
  • Ball. ball.csv has one ball per frame. Filter by score before using it. ball_3d_on_field.csv keeps the ball inside the pitch.
  • Vitruvian re-fit. Frames whose camera is a homography, a planar fit or nothing get their height from the people's boxes (bottom centre = ground, top centre = head, 1.83 m). The result goes to vitruvian.dlt3d and vitruvian_diagnostics.csv. The run's own .dlt3d is not changed, and those frames keep the default SAM 3D Body focal. Check the residual before trusting a refit.
  • Pitch-frame 3D. Everything is in metres on the pitch (origin at the centre, +x to the right goal, +y to the far touchline, +z up), from the run's .dlt3d. field_kps_3d.csv has the 49 field points per frame with the reprojection error; poses_3d.csv or poses_3d_on_field.csv the keypoints of each person, rotated into the field and anchored with the feet on the ground under the box; ball_3d.csv and ball_3d_on_field.csv the ball on the ground plane at its radius height; field_ball.c3d and person_<id>.c3d (flat in the output folder) the same markers for a C3D viewer (one file per person; a C3D file holds at most 255 marker names). Caveat: the pose height is smaller than the box. On the test clip the nose is about 1.4 m above the anchored feet, against 1.8 m expected; the horizontal placement agrees with the box.

Outputs go to <run>/detect3d/ (the separate detect-3d command) or to the flat folder of Detect + 3D, which also writes the full overlay video <video>_3d_overlay.mp4. Needs CUDA and the SAM 3 / SAM 3D Body weights.

Load previous result. Section 5 has Load previous result...: pick a finished <video>_3d_<time> folder (or a detect run or batch). It fills the video, the output folder, the model, the detect options, Players on pitch only and the ball choice from that result, and Correct in getpixelvideo, Ball track and Rebuild ball then use that result instead of the newest folder. Physics of the corrected ball. A hand-corrected ball follows a fitted flight: drag with the Brazuca Cd(Re), Magnus lift from the spin, and the spin inferred from the pixels. Pass the three physical anchors with --ball-anchors '{"kicker_id": 30, "barrier_id": 15, "keeper_id": 21}' (the player IDs of the clip). The fit and its residuals go to ball_flight.json, and <video>_flight_check.mp4 shows the fitted ball over the video: marked pixels in yellow, the fitted ball in red, the anchors as crosses. The anchors field is in section 5, next to Players on pitch only; leave it empty to keep the smoothed track. The full explanation, with the equations, the sources and the limits of this result, is in docs/fisica_bola_freekiki.pdf (HTML beside it).

Ball anchors: the dialog (Ball anchors...). Open it after Detect + 3D (it reads the run's ball and players). It starts with three rows: the ball on the ground at the first frame (the kick), the ball in the air at the middle frame (height σ 0.8 m: you know it little), and the last ball position on the goal line. Each row says "the ball was at this point, in this frame". The source can be the ball itself (on the ground or with a height z), a keypoint of a kept player (choose the ID and the body part), or a point of the goal (centre on the ground or at the crossbar, a post, on the goal line at x = ±52,45 m). σ is how much you trust the point in metres: the larger it is, the less the fit obeys it. At least three rows are needed. Save writes the JSON into the Ball anchors field; then Rebuild ball uses it. The JSON accepts these points, {"points": [{"frame": 0, "xyz": [x, y, z], "sigma": 0.2, "label": "start"}, ...]}, or the player form (kicker_id, barrier_id, keeper_id) of the earlier runs.

Report for the press (report-tv). Needs ball_flight.json in the 3D folder — written only when Rebuild ball runs with Ball anchors filled (≥3 points via Ball anchors..., or player-id JSON). A rebuild without anchors updates the track only; TV Report then asks you to set anchors and rebuild. For a finished run with that file, the section 5 button TV Report (or freekiki.py report-tv --run <3D folder> --names "<player>" --authors "<team>") writes report_tv/ inside the run, with one folder per language: pt/ (relatorio_bola.html, relatorio_bola.pdf, animacao_lance.mp4) and en/ (ball_report.html, ball_report.pdf, kick_animation.mp4), each with its figures in figuras/. --lang pt, --lang en or --lang both (the default) chooses the languages; --title and --subtitle replace the default title of both. The report explains the physics in plain words, gives the numbers of the run, the references, the limits of the result and ready quotes. The animation is the broadcast in slow motion, with the fitted ball, the forces on it and the anchors drawn over it. The numbers come from the run's files only. The confidence warning at the top follows the anchor residuals: a low result is an estimate, and the text says so. A binocular folder has no poses of its own, so the people of the top view (the player ids of the kicker, the wall and the goalkeeper) come from the reference camera's 3D folder named in its summary.json.

Editor in the report (HTML). At the bottom of each HTML, the anchors are a table (point, frame, x, y, z, σ). Change them and click Recompute: the fit runs in the browser, with the same model as the physics module, and the panel shows the error in the image, the deviation at each anchor, the kick time, the spin, the peak speed and the distance. The text of the report does not change (it comes from the original run). Download JSON saves the anchors for Rebuild ball. Use --no-animation for the report alone.

Top view and people. The top view of the report shows the goal area, the kicker, the two wall players nearest the ball at the wall frame (the green segment between them) and the goalkeeper with his path. The ids are found by the nearest point, and the report says which ids it used. --kicker-id, --wall-ids 15,2 and --keeper-id replace them. In a penalty the top view names only the penalty taker and the goalkeeper, and draws the goalkeeper's hands at the kick and in the last frame. The animation draws every point of the flight with the camera of the frame on screen, so the start of the path stays on the kicker's foot while the broadcast camera pans.

Two cameras: binocular ball (rec3d-binocular). The monocular 3D run places the ball on the ground plane, so its height is not measured. With two synchronized cameras of the same kick, freekiki.py rec3d-binocular --run1 <3D folder of camera 1> --run2 <3D folder of camera 2> triangulates the ball from the two pixel tracks and the per-frame DLT3D of each camera. It writes processed_rec3d_binocular_<time>/ with ball_binocular.csv (the ball in metres per frame, the reprojection error in each camera, the angle between the rays, and a valid flag with its reason) and summary.json (counts, medians, the clock used, and the comparison with the monocular track). The sync comes from the file names (*_sync_<n>_frames_<a>_to_<b>). The default clock of each camera is frame / fps of its cut video. --timing1 and --timing2 give the original file whose capture times to use instead; the summary then reports both clocks. A frame is valid only when both views reproduce the ball within 3 px and the rays make at least 5 degrees (--max-resid, --min-angle). These limits are operational choices, not validated thresholds. The result is a 3D estimate from one pair of views, not a laboratory measurement. Its fit is written to ball_flight.json with method: binocular, and report-tv says in the report that the height comes from two cameras.

Monocular ball: automatic start and arrival. When the run has no manual anchors, the single-camera flight is fitted from the kick contact: the first frame in which the ball moves more than 5 px from its rest pixel. The still ball before it gives the rest point on the ground, and the flight is not fitted before the contact. The arrival is the goal entry when the ball crosses the goal plane (x = ±52.45 m) inside the goal mouth. The entry point is the ball on that plane, mapped with the goal homography of pynalty_analysis from the four goal corners projected by the frame's DLT3D. Otherwise (a save) the arrival is the goalkeeper's hands in the last frame. The record is ball_flight.json with method: monocular_auto. Checked on two clips of the same kick against the binocular track: the contact agreed within about 0.15 m, but in flight the single-camera track drifted (median 3D difference of 1.4 m for the 16 mm camera and 3.5 m for the main camera), the goal entry point differed by about 4 m laterally, and the entry or save call changed between the cameras. Treat a monocular arrival as a rough estimate, and use the binocular pair when it exists.

One click: the whole kick analysis (kick-analysis). The button One-click kick analysis in section 5, or freekiki.py kick-analysis -w <workspace> --videos <folder or the two videos>. The folder must hold exactly two cut videos whose names carry the sync (..._sync_<n>_frames_<a>_to_<b>), or the two videos written by the event equalizer (Make Sync file → Equalize by events (RIFE)) with their event_equalizer.json beside them: those names carry no sync, but both videos share one grid, so the contact frame of the manifest (the middle event, counted from 0) is the sync of both, and the equalizer's reference camera is camera 1 (--reference 2 swaps them). Each video needs its <video>_markers.csv (the ball marked or corrected in getpixelvideo; the equalized videos are new files, so the ball has to be marked on them). When a file is missing the run stops before detecting anything and lists every camera without it, with the getpixelvideo.py -f <video> command that writes it. A folder with one video whose name has no sync (the cut tool's ..._frames_<a>_to_<b>) is the monocular analysis of that video, and it needs its <video>_markers.csv too; the button sends the file itself when a single video is chosen, and the folder when a cut video of a pair is chosen. One camera alone needs no sync for its monocular analyses (they read only its DLT3D; a video with no sync anywhere takes 0, and --sync with --cut-start beats the equalizer's, not the name's). A _markers.csv with 49 points is refused: it is the keypoints file under the ball's name, and the keypoints go in <video>_kiki49.csv. A camera with its <video>_kiki49.csv uses those hand marks instead of the screen. The run writes one new folder kick_analysis_<time>/ next to the videos, in this order: the keypoints of each camera screened (a camera the network does not recognise stops the run, unless it has hand marks), the DLT3D of every frame, SAM 3 + DINOv3 (SAM 3D Body) with the corrected ball, the people kept on the pitch (the pitch filter is always on here, because the player link reads poses_3d_on_field.csv) and paired between the two cameras (player_map.csv), the ball in metres from the two cameras (processed_rec3d_binocular_<time>/, with report_tv/), the keypoints of the paired people triangulated with the DLT3D of each frame (poses_3d_binocular.csv; on the weak frames the Vitruvian refit replaces the run's DLT), the kicker's metrics (kicker_metrics.csv and .json, kicker_series.csv, kicker_sensitivity.csv, the figures of the next paragraph) and README.txt. pipeline_status.json records every stage with its inputs and outputs. Running again with --out <that folder> reuses the finished stages; --no-reuse runs them all again. The kicker's metrics are: foot speed before contact (mean over about 33 ms of the foot marker, the mean of big-toe-tip, small-toe-tip and heel); ball speed after contact (the fitted flight, about 33 ms); their ratio Vball/Vfoot (after Vieira et al. 2018); the distance from the support foot to the ball at contact; the approach angle (hip displacement before contact against the ball's direction after it; an operational definition, not a threshold); the knee angle and its extension speed; the peak angular speeds of thigh, shank and foot before contact and whether they come in proximal-distal order; and the goal error (the fitted entry on the goal plane against the centre of the goal mouth). Each number states its source (observado = triangulated keypoint, filtrado = local quadratic fit, ajustado = flight fit, inferido = from two of these). A metric without its points is left empty with the reason. Limits: at 59.94 fps the contact lasts about 1 ms, so the speed at impact is not measured; the 33 ms windows are not the 10 frames at 300 Hz of Vieira et al. (2018), so the absolute values are not comparable; the 2D keypoints are reprojections of each camera's own SAM 3D Body mesh, so the triangulated points fuse two models, and the residuals in poses_3d_binocular.csv are the quality control; the foot marker is not the fifth metatarsal; the knee angle is a three-point angle, not an anatomical Euler angle; stature is the 1.83 m prior and the height check is only a control. Attack angle, impact point and last stride length are not in this version.

Player height. The SAM 3D Body skeleton comes out about 1.2 to 1.5 m tall in its own units. Each track is scaled vertically so that its median nose sits at 1.73 m: a 1.83 m stature prior minus an assumed 0.10 m from the nose to the top of the head. The prior is the average stature given for the players, not a measurement of each one. The feet stay on the ground and the x-y positions do not change. A track whose median nose is below 0.2 m is not scaled, and a factor outside 0.8 to 1.6 is flagged. The values before and after the scale, per person, are in stature_by_person.csv.

Set piece: penalty, free kick with a wall, or free kick (setpiece). Before the analysis, the run decides what kind of ball at rest it is, from the reference camera's 3D folder: the ball's position at the first frame on the pitch and the feet of the people. A penalty: the ball is 11 m from the goal line (within 1.5 m), within 3.0 m of the middle of the goal, and fewer than two people have their feet 8.5 to 10.0 m from the ball, between it and the goal. A free kick with a wall: two or more people in that ring. Any other ball at rest is a free kick. The attacked goal is the one nearer the ball. The wall is listed in the run's player ids (keeper 0, kicker 1, the others 2, 3, ...; wall_sam_ids keeps the SAM ids). A single clip whose name has no sync numbers takes --sync (the frame where the ball is touched) and --cut-start (the first frame of the cut); the name wins when it has them. The result is data/setpiece.json in each analysis folder and the line set piece of README.txt. Detect + 3D also judges it, from the ball of that run, and prints a provisional line (set piece (provisional, ball of this run = …)) with the file data/setpiece_provisional.json in the 3D folder. That ball is not corrected by hand (a SAM track can be wrong), so the One-click analysis, which reads the corrected ball, is the one that counts: its last line says set piece: …. With --ball off there is no ball and the kind is unknown. The Set piece radios below Result of the one-click analysis force the kind — Auto (geral) / Penalty / Free kick (auto, penalty or free_kick; CLI --set-piece): a forced free kick never goes to the penalty metrics, and a forced penalty with no ball at rest is reported as unavailable. The rule's own answer stays in data/setpiece.json as auto_kind. A free kick (with or without a wall) keeps the analysis described above. A penalty has no team or referee split: its only people are the keeper (ID0) and the kicker (ID1); the other people's poses are written to data/other_IDs/ and nothing reads them. In the top view, in the _3d_overlay.mp4 video (redrawn with these ids) and in the report the keeper is ID0 and the kicker is ID1, the others 2, 3, and a penalty has no wall. A penalty does not get the freekiki ball report: the pynalty metrics of the chosen analysis go to data/penalty_metrics.csv and .json, and the pynalty report (English and Portuguese) to report_tv/penalty/. The pynalty metrics come from the reference camera's pixels: the four goal-mouth posts projected by the camera's DLT at the frame nearest the kick, the ball path of the next paragraph (its goal frame), and the keeper's SAM box centre and pose (the MHR70 keypoints mapped to the 33 pynalty landmarks; the missing ones are left empty). Limits: the distances and the ring are operational choices, not validated thresholds; the keeper point is the box centre, not the point marked by hand in the pynalty app, so the values are not the app's; a penalty whose fitted flight does not reach the goal line within the clip, or with the keeper not seen at the kick or at the goal, is reported as not available with its reason; a free kick without a wall still shows the barrier section of the freekiki report (removing it is not done yet); the ball path has been checked against the binocular fit of one real penalty (see the next paragraph).

Penalty ball path (penalty_path.csv). For a penalty the ball's path is the flight of freekiki_physics.fit_flight (gravity, drag and Magnus spin) fitted to the ball's pixels of the clip, with one anchor: the spot, the penalty mark 11 m from the goal line on the centre line, known exactly, where the ball rests at the first frame of the record. The goal frame is the first frame in which the fitted ball is across the goal line. When the clip does not get there, the penalty metrics are not available and the README says so. data/penalty_path.csv holds the fitted points frame by frame: x_m, y_m, z_m, horiz_dev_m (the distance on the ground from the spot-arrival line) and bow_left_m (the signed offset to the kicker's left, facing the goal). The path is straight when no point of the flight is more than 0.30 m from that line on the ground, and curved otherwise; a curved path bows to the side named in the README (bow_side). The vertical arc of a lob is not a curve: it is reported as the apex height (apex_z_m). The ray of the last pixel met with the goal line is written as ray_arrival_m for comparison only: it is not an anchor, because the ball is not on the goal line in that frame in general. The README says how far the ball at rest sits from the spot (spot_fit_offset_m): about 0.16 m on both monocular clips checked, which points at the calibration or at the ball's placement. With two cameras the verdict is the binocular ball (the source of the path is binocular; the Bruno run gives straight at 0.24 m); with one camera it is the monocular fit, labelled provisional. Checked on the Bruno penalty against its binocular fit (two cameras, the same kick): the monocular track stays within 0.37 m on average (0.64 m at most) of the binocular ball, and both are straight at the 0.30 m limit (0.29 m and 0.27 m). On the Roseguine clip (one camera, 19 frames) the verdict depends on the model: 0.18 m (straight) with a ray anchor at the last frame, 0.31 m (curved) with the spot as the only anchor, which is the one used now. So a monocular straight or curved verdict near 0.30 m is not reliable: a second camera decides it. Limits: the 0.30 m limit is a provisional choice, not validated on many kicks; with one camera the depth of the ball is weak, so the bend depends on the fit; and the clip may end before the goal line. The goalkeeper's move is reported as a partial measure (`gk_partial` in `penalty_metrics.csv`): from the contact of the fitted flight, the first frame in which the middle of the hips is 0.10 m from where the goalkeeper stood, the side the goalkeeper moves to (the goalkeeper's right or left: the goalkeeper faces the kicker, so the goalkeeper's right is the kicker's left; toward_right_m is positive to the goalkeeper's right), the displacement and the mean speed up to the last frame. It does not report a dive, because the dive is measured up to the goal line. A penalty has no team or referee split, and it has the freekiki report in `report_tv/` (the ball report, without the wall), with the kicker and the goalkeeper in its figures. Its texts are the penalty's (no wall paragraph, no time to the wall, "last frame" where a free kick says "entry into the goal", because the ball may not reach the goal line within the clip). The figures and the slow-motion animation show the goalkeeper's hands, the mean of the two wrists (the pose model has no hand point), at the kick (open circle, "goalkeeper's hands: start") and in the last frame (filled circle, "goalkeeper's hands: end"), with the distance between the two positions and the distance from the last hands to the ball; the animation banner reads kick, ball in flight, last frame. The hands carry the error of a single camera: a distance to the ball of the order of 0.5 m does not tell a touch from a near miss. The goalkeeper's dive is discussed against the literature in the report's references (section 8): Monteiro et al. 2022 and 2024 (Scientific Reports, goalkeepers' penalty dive kinematics), Navarro et al. 2013 (the goalkeeper's presence and the accuracy of penalty kicks), and Attaallah et al. 2021 (penalty kick accuracy, a biomechanical approach).

Kick figures and curves (kick-figures). A kick-analysis folder also holds the kicker's stick figures and curves, in Portuguese and English. kicker_sticks_side_{pt,en}.png and kicker_sticks_top_{pt,en}.png show six instants from 50 ms before the contact to 33 ms after it (side view: x = direction of the kick, z = height; top view: x, y). The goalkeeper is drawn in every stick view, in green. Two front views on the plane (y, z) come with them: kicker_sticks_front_goal_{pt,en}.png from behind the goal (the goalkeeper's view, looking at the kicker) and kicker_sticks_front_kicker_{pt,en}.png from behind the kicker (looking at the goal); the lateral axis is the observer's right, so the two are mirror images. The goal side comes from the set piece (goal_x_m in data/setpiece.json) and, without it, from the direction of the ball after the contact; when neither is known the two front views are not drawn, because a guess would mirror them. The centre of mass (CoM) of the kicker and of the goalkeeper is a diamond in every stick view and in the interactive page, and is written to data/com_players.csv (frame, role, player_id, x_m, y_m, z_m): the segment model of De Leva (1996) with the mass fractions of the deadlift module, each segment taken at its midpoint (an operational definition, not De Leva's own segment centres), the hands being the wrists; it is NaN when the trunk is missing or less than 60% of the body mass is present. The kicker's contact frame is the one of the fitted flight when its image error is below 3 px and it differs by more than one frame from the first movement of the hand-marked ball (contact_frame with source ajustado in kicker_metrics.csv, which names the marker's frame); otherwise it is the marker's. kicker_curves_{pt,en}.png aligns the speeds, the knee angle and extension, the segment angular speeds, the pelvis and limb angles and the knee's share of the foot speed to the contact, in the layout of Levanon & Dapena (1998). kicker_overlay_{ref,other}_{pt,en}.png reprojects the triangulated keypoints onto the contact frame of each camera, with the median and maximum residual of that camera: this is the visual check that the triangulation sits on the athlete. kicker_interactive_{pt,en}.html is a self-contained page with a frame slider over the four stick views (side, top, behind the goal and behind the kicker, with the goalkeeper) and the curves. freekiki.py kick-figures <kick_analysis folder> (or the Kick figures button in section 5) rewrites these files from the run's own files, without detecting or triangulating again, and keeps the kicking foot the run chose. The new quantities are operational definitions, not the paper's: pelvis rotation (the hip line against the kick direction, signed and folded into −90° to 90°, at contact); the angle between the planes hip–knee–ankle and knee–ankle–toe (the toe is the big toe, or the small toe where the big one is missing); the hip angle (shoulder–hip–knee) and the ankle angle (knee–ankle–toe), both three-point angles; the lateral foot speed (across the kick direction, mean over the pre-contact window); and the knee's share of the foot speed, the velocity that the knee's own rotation gives the foot, projected on the foot's velocity and divided by its speed (a kinematic decomposition, not inverse dynamics). A speed that changes by more than half between the 33 ms and 67 ms windows is marked window-sensitive in kicker_metrics.csv. Limits: the paper was read from its abstract only, so the definitions above are not checked against its figures; at 59.94 fps the sequence has steps of 16.7 ms; the kicking foot's toes and ankle drop out of the triangulation around the contact, so the plane and ankle angles and the knee share are often empty there; the angular speeds are noisy in this run and are shown as they are.

Binocular C3D. The field points (49, FKP_<name>) and the ball are in processed_rec3d_binocular_<time>/data/field_ball.c3d. The field points lie on the ground, so each is solved in x and y from the accepted 2D points of the two cameras with the DLT3D of each frame, and z is 0; a point is kept when both cameras reproduce it within 10 px. The players are in the team folders, one P<id>_<role>.c3d per player, with the 70 MHR keypoints triangulated in freekiki_pose_binocular. A person seen in one camera only cannot be triangulated: it is not in the C3D files.

Teams and roles. Each person is labelled from the colour inside the box of its SAM track, on the torso only. TEAM_A is the shirt colour you name (--team-a, green in this clip). TEAM_B is the other colour that most field players wear, counted on the pitch and not the goalkeeper. The referees wear the colour left over, and the goalkeeper has his own colour. The goalkeeper is in the team opposite to the kicker. Roles come from player_map.csv: 0 is the goalkeeper and 1 the kicker; the orange people are REF_MAIN (the nearest to the ball at the first frame), REF_AUX1 and REF_AUX2. At most 11 players per team (goalkeeper included) and 3 referees; the extra people are not assigned, and the reason is written. A camera alone sees only its own shirts: a person whose torso is less than 15 % one colour is not assigned in that analysis (the binocular one, with both cameras, may assign him). The height is estimated from camera 1, whose vertical scale is checked on the goal posts (2.44 m, within 0.4 px): the head is the nose plus 10 cm (an assumption), the feet are the heels on the ground, and only upright frames count. A value outside 1.40 to 2.10 m is not shown. It is an estimate: there is no measured height of the players to check it against.

Player ids across cameras (players-link). Field geometry (fill / fix) — AI + mathematics

The pitch is a known 3D object (soccerfield_kiki.csv, metres, z up). In every frame FreeKiki fits a camera to the accepted keypoints, then predicts where every other keypoint must be.

The camera:

  • a planar homography (RANSAC, refined with Hartley-normalised SVD) of the z = 0 points;
  • a DLT3D (11 parameters computed via Hartley-normalised SVD, as in dlt3d.py) when ≥ 7 points including ≥ 2 off the ground (flags 1.5 m, post tops 2.44 m) are found;
  • otherwise a pinhole camera recovered from the homography: Zhang focal refined on the points, centred principal point, square pixels;
  • temporal regularisation: within each contiguous shot (never crossing scene cuts), normalised Ht and Pt camera matrices are smoothed with a rolling window (when inlier RMSE stays within threshold) to suppress inter-frame jitter.

Select frames in video: find the rare keypoints in long games

For downloaded games: select detects at the normal keypoint confidence (the workspace setting, 0.5), finds the moments where the field camera puts the rare points (p5, p29, p39, p47) in the picture or the network sees them, and cuts those moments out with the vailá Cut Video ffmpeg cutter. The short clips are what you then label.

Close-up cameras are dropped. The network is trained on wide cameras: a camera behind the goal, on a celebrating player or zoomed in makes it put keypoints in wrong places. The pose gate runs MediaPipe Pose (the PoseLandmarker of markerless_2d_analysis.py) every 10 frames of each candidate window; a pose taller than 1/10 of the image height is a close-up. The window is split there and only the stretches without close-ups are kept. No pose found means the frame is apparently fine (players of a wide shot are small). A camera also needs at least 6 accepted points to mark a frame.

Camera check. Sorting the first 56 clips by hand showed the network is confident on a camera behind the net or a zoom (it accepts more points there, 21 against 17 on average) but those points do not lie on the pitch plane seen from a plausible camera. A frame with at least 6 accepted points is judged: the fitted camera must have an rmse of at most 5 px (at 1920 px width) and stand 8-45 m high. A frame that fails makes no hit and splits its window (--max-rmse 5, --cam-height 8,45).

Heatmap backend (vailá-native, no company-owned framework)

train --backend heatmap trains a second kind of network on the same labels (datasets/kiki49, no rebuild):

  • HRNet-W32 (--backbone hrnet_w32): Sun et al. (CVPR 2019) multi-scale architecture keeping parallel high-to-low streams (strides 4, 8, 16, 32 with 32, 64, 128, 256 channels) alive throughout, fused bilinearly into 480 stride-4 channels + 1×1 conv → 49 heatmaps (slot hm_hr32).
  • ResNet (--backbone resnet18/34/50/101/152): torchvision trunk (ImageNet weights from local cache) + 3 deconvolutions → 49 heatmaps (slots hm_n..hm_x).

Knowledge Distillation (L = Lgt + λ Ldistill)

YOLO weights cannot be copied directly into HRNet; transfer is performed via knowledge distillation (--teacher active, --distill-weight 0.5):

L = Lgt + 0.5 · Ldistill

  • Lgt: MSE against ground-truth heatmaps (v=0 produces a zero map).
  • Ldistill: MSE against soft Gaussian heatmaps emitted by the frozen YOLO teacher on the exact same augmented canvas on GPU.
  • Where v=0 in ground truth but teacher confidence ≥ 0.2, the student inherits the teacher's prediction (soft Gaussian with peak = teacher confidence).
