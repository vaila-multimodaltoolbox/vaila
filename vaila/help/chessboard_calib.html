Chessboard Calib v0.4.15

Calibrates several cameras from chessboard videos (one video per camera, same session) and exports the files that vailá needs for 3D reconstruction.

Module: vaila/chessboard_calib.py
Button: Frame C-A → Chessboard Calib (below DLT/REC 2D-3D)
Category: Processing · Version: 0.4.15 · Updated: 08 November 2026 · Author: Paulo Santiago · License: AGPL-3.0

Pipeline

  1. Corners. Every --step-th frame is searched. While the board was seen recently, only an upscaled crop around it is searched (--zoom, default 2); otherwise the full frame every --search-every frames. Corners are refined to sub-pixel accuracy and numbered from the top-left corner of the upright board, so a physical corner has the same number in every camera.
    Frame numbers are video timestamps (round(t × fps), first frame 0), as in getpixelvideo and in a constant-frame-rate copy. Network cameras sometimes drop frames (gaps in the timestamps), and a frame after a gap may also fail to decode. Those numbers are missing and are listed per camera in the log, the report (frame_numbering) and the README. Counting frames while reading would shift every later frame by the number of drops, differently in each camera. When an MP4 reports a lower average rate only because of the drops, the nominal rate (r_frame_rate) is used.
  2. Intrinsics per camera. With --intrinsics auto the model depends on the image coverage of the corners: ≥ 30 % standard (f, principal point, k1, k2, p1, p2); 5–30 % constrained (f, k1, k2); < 5 % minimal (f, k1).
    Board tilt: the report gives, per camera, how much the board orientation varied (board_tilt_p95_deg). Below 20° the board kept almost one orientation, the focal length cannot be told apart from the board distance, and the log and README print a WARNING. The validation does not see it, because it measures the board itself.
    Loading the lens: --intrinsics-toml-dir takes a folder of <camera>_intrinsics.toml, for example the processed_chessboard_calib_* folder of a calibration of the same cameras where the board moved. Each video takes the TOML whose camera name starts or is contained in its name, so cut (<camera>_sync_..._frames_...) and equalized (source_2_<camera>_..._equalized) copies use the lens of the original camera. The log names the file used and warns when none matches. Close-up videos can be passed with --intrinsics-input.
  3. Frame offsets (--sync auto, needs --step 1): the board's rotation between frames has the same angle in every camera; its cross-correlation gives the lag between cameras. Each lag is refined to a fraction of a frame (parabola through the correlation peak). The offsets are solved together and rounded to whole frames; the values before rounding are reported (frame_offsets_subframe). Cameras that are not genuinely synchronized differ by fractions of a frame, which a whole-frame shift cannot remove. Convention: camera frame = reference frame + offset. Manual: --frame-offsets, which may have decimals (e.g. offsets measured in a recording where the board moved). The decimals are kept for --subframe and the RIFE command.
    The sub-frame offsets need a moving board. On the lab's frontal clip, with the board held still, the estimate was off by 0.1–0.2 frame. On a segment where the board moved, a recalibration after whole-frame shifts recovered the expected remainder within 0.015 frame.
    Fractions of a frame can be used in two ways. With undistort --subframe, the pixel CSVs are interpolated in time. Alternatively, the videos themselves are aligned with RIFE: when a camera is more than 0.1 frame off and no camera dropped frames, the log and README print a ready Video Event Equalizer command with decimal events.
  4. Extrinsics: planar PnP per view, initial camera poses from shared views, then a bundle adjustment of all camera and board poses with fixed intrinsics (views above max(3 × median, 2 px) are removed once).
  5. World frame: --world board (origin at corner 0 of the board in the frame seen by most cameras; with --board-axes upright X along the rows, Z up, Y through the board) or --world control (CSV name,x,y,z in metres plus one vailá pixel CSV per camera with the same points).
  6. Checks: reprojection error per camera; board corners triangulated with the exported DLTs and compared with the square size, in the calibration frames and in held-out 60-frame blocks (--holdout-block).

Recording the calibration videos

  • Show the board to every camera, close and far, over the whole image including edges and corners. Lens distortion is measured only where the board appeared.
  • Tilt the board 30–45° in several directions. A board held still in one place gives a focal length that trades against distance. An example is the lab's frontal clip of the board standing on the force plate, with a tilt of 7–8°. There cameras 06/07/08 got f = 1284 / 1195 / 989 px instead of 1075 / 1125 / 1055 px, and points 2–3 m from the board were 58 mm (median) away from the 8-camera calibration, while the clip's own validation read 0.76 mm. If such a clip defines the world frame, load the lens of a calibration where the board moved (--intrinsics-toml-dir).
  • Keep the board upright (rows within ±45° of horizontal).
  • Several cameras should see the board at the same moments, forming one connected chain.
  • Measure the square side over several squares with a ruler: it sets the scale of everything. The lab board has 98 mm squares (--square 0.098, the default); 0.1 instead of 0.098 makes 1 m read 1.020 m.

After calibration: 3D reconstruction

  1. Remove the lens distortion with this calibration (each CSV name starts with or contains its camera video name): 
    uv run vaila/chessboard_calib.py undistort --calib out/processed_chessboard_calib_YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS -i pixels/ --apply-offsets
    In the GUI: Chessboard Calib → Undistort pixels for Rec3D.... --apply-offsets rewrites the frame column to the reference timeline (frame − offset). The offsets were measured in the calibration recording, so they apply only to pixel CSVs of those same recordings; measure them again for a new recording.
    --subframe (GUI: answer yes to “Use the sub-frame offsets”) uses the offsets before rounding: each camera's value at reference frame n is interpolated linearly between its frames ⌊n + e⌋ and ⌈n + e⌉. A missing or empty neighbour gives an empty value, so gaps and dropped frames are not filled. An integer offset gives exactly --apply-offsets. Without fractional offsets in the report (--sync none, manual offsets), --subframe stops with an error. Pixel CSVs of videos already aligned with the Video Event Equalizer need neither option.
    The CSV frame numbers must follow the video timestamps, as getpixelvideo and this module do. Tools that count frames while reading (Markerless 2D, Video to PNG) number every frame after a dropped frame differently. If the report lists dropped frames, run them on constant-frame-rate copies (ffmpeg -i <camera>.mp4 -fps_mode cfr -r 30 -c:v libx264 -crf 12 <camera>_cfr.mp4), where a dropped frame repeats the previous image.
  2. Run Rec3D 1DLT with the .dlt3d files and the undistorted CSVs; step 1 prints the exact command.
Do not use Distort CSV before Rec3D with these files. It reprojects with cv2.getOptimalNewCameraMatrix at a fixed 1920×1080, a different camera matrix from the one in the .dlt3d files.

Verified on the lab videos (8 cameras; 06 and 08 November 2026). All 8 cameras lost 2–3 frames at the same moment, about 14.6 s into the recording.

  • Offsets: with timestamp numbering, the offsets of cameras 07 and 08 relative to camera 06 are the same before and after the drop (−3 and +5). Counting frames gave −3/−4 and +5/+4.
  • 8 cameras, 98 mm squares: bundle adjustment 0.47 px; board triangulation 0.76 mm median (p95 1.16 mm), held-out blocks 0.77 mm (p95 1.20 mm); board tilt 54–70° in every camera. The earlier run with 0.1 m had the same lens and every coordinate 2 % larger.
  • Rec3D 1DLT, cameras 06/07/08 (board corners, 352 frames): 0.74 mm median with --apply-offsets, 0.69 mm with --subframe. In the 10 % of frames where the board moved fastest: 0.70 → 0.62 mm median, p95 0.85 → 0.72 mm.
  • Frontal clip (cameras 06/07/08, board standing still on the force plate). Compared with the 8-camera calibration:

CLI

uv run vaila/chessboard_calib.py                                   # GUI
uv run vaila/chessboard_calib.py -i videos/ --pattern 6x8 --square 0.098
uv run vaila/chessboard_calib.py -i cut_videos/ --intrinsics-toml-dir out/processed_chessboard_calib_YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS
uv run vaila/chessboard_calib.py undistort --calib out/processed_chessboard_calib_YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS -i pixels/ --subframe
uv run vaila/chessboard_calib.py -i videos/ -o out/ --step 1 --zoom 2 --sync auto
uv run vaila/chessboard_calib.py -i videos/ --reuse-detections out/processed_chessboard_calib_YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS \
    --world control --control-points floor_points.csv --control-pixels floor_pixels/

On NVIDIA/CUDA workstations use uv run --no-sync. All options: uv run vaila/chessboard_calib.py --help and vaila/help/chessboard_calib.md.

Limits

  • With wide-angle lenses and a board that stays near the image centre, the distortion near the image border is extrapolated; points reconstructed there are less accurate than the validation numbers suggest.
  • Both checks use the same session and board: they show that the cameras agree with each other and with the board size, not accuracy against a reference system.
  • A board that keeps one orientation does not determine the focal length (see Board tilt); the validation cannot detect it.
  • Offsets measured by --sync auto come from the board's motion; with a board that hardly moves they are uncertain by about 0.1–0.2 frame.
  • --apply-offsets uses whole frames: cameras that are not genuinely synchronized keep up to half a frame between them (17 ms at 30 fps). --subframe and the RIFE alignment reduce it, but their in-between values are estimates, not measurements.
  • The fisheye model is not offered; the exported TOML and the undistortion use the standard OpenCV model.

Tests

uv run pytest tests/test_chessboard_calib.py tests/test_video_timestamp_numbering.py tests/test_video_event_equalizer.py -v

Synthetic cameras and boards with known truth. They include an end-to-end run on rendered videos with one camera starting 3 frames late, a lag of a fraction of a frame, and a video with dropped frames. The dropped-frame test checks timestamp numbers in the detection and in getpixelvideo, paused and playing. Further tests cover the lens loaded by camera name, the board-tilt warning, --subframe (linear motion recovered exactly, gaps left empty), the RIFE command and decimal events in the equalizer.
